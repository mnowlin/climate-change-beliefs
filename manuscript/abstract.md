# abstract

Climate change is one of the most polarized issues among the US public. Previous research has identified several factors that influence the public's views on climate change, including cultural worldviews, political beliefs, environmental orientation, views on the scientific consensus, and solution-aversion (i.e., opposition to climate policy solutions). Beliefs about climate change are interconnected and form a belief system. Recent work has noted that the interconnected beliefs in a belief system can be understood as a network. In a belief system network, beliefs are the nodes, and edges represent correlations between beliefs, and the focus is on the centrality of beliefs within the network. Using a quota-based sample of the US public, I explore the belief system network associated with climate change. Examining the major network centrality measures - betweenness, closeness, and strength - I find that cultural worldviews, particularly egalitarianism, support for climate policies, particularly EPA regulations, and the perception of risk associated with climate change are the most central beliefs in the network. These findings support the importance of cultural worldviews in shaping beliefs, as well as the nature of solution-aversion regarding climate change.
